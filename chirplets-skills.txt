Computation of some projective-chirplet-transform and metaplectic-chirplet-transform subspaces, with applications in image processing
Steve Mann and Shawn Becker, DSP World Symposium, Boston, November 1992

Time-frequency and chirplet analysis
- Chirplet transform: decomposition into pieces whose frequency can change over time, rather than fixed-frequency wavelet packets
- Time-frequency analysis of 1D signals and 2D images
- Linear chirps and projective chirps, where spatial frequency increases or decreases over distance
- Quadratic phase modulation
- Computing selected subspaces of a higher-dimensional chirplet parameter space instead of the full transform

Projective chirplet transform (PCT)
- Projective geometry and camera perspective
- Projective group SL(3, R)
- Perspective tilt as a spatial-frequency chirp (grid lines compressing toward a horizon)
- Restricted coordinates such as horizontal and vertical tilts and pan-tilt-zoom paths
- Image rectification and perspective correction, including un-warping a skewed document or billboard
- Horizon tracking
- Vanishing points and vanishing lines
- Camera orientation relative to a ground plane without tracking discrete landmarks

Metaplectic chirplet transform (MCT)
- Metaplectic group as a double cover of the symplectic group
- Links among signal processing, optics, and the Wigner distribution
- Linear chirps from spatial shear, rotation, and quadratic phase
- Localized orientation and acceleration of image components
- Motion-blur removal
- Doppler and phase-shift models for objects moving across a sensor

Imaging applications
- Tracking how objects deform, rotate, or change perspective relative to a camera
- Synthetic aperture radar (SAR), radar, and sonar: target velocity and acceleration smearing, and de-blurring fast-moving objects
- Medical imaging: shear and other continuous non-rigid tissue deformation in ultrasound and MRI, including elasticity
- Biometrics: normalizing elastic skin stretch and camera-to-eye tilt in iris and fingerprint images before matching
